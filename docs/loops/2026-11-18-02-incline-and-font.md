## The questions as answered

**What should change.** A block whose text is leaned has to be drawn leaned, at the
size of the glyphs it replaces. The two blocks on the 四十歲 page are the report:
the TV speech at `320,580 73x135` and the 卒業証書 banner at `202,983 146x376`.
Their labels were upright, and the font came from the axis-aligned box width, so a
22 px glyph got a 57 px label and a 45 px glyph got a 101 px one.

**How to verify.** `npm run regression -- incline`, a new case on
`fixtures/page-forty.jpg`. It measures the labels actually drawn in the two
regions, not what the reader said:

- the label's rotation within ±4° of the ink's lean (5° and 15.5°);
- the label's font within ±30% of the source glyph (22 px and 48 px);
- no two labels in one region may cover more than 40% of one another.

The rendered page is `out/regression/incline.png`; the guards are the other nine
cases and `npm run typecheck`.

**Done when.** Both blocks pass, the nine other cases pass, and the case fails on
the pre-fix code. Budget: 5 rounds, because the check had to be written, the reader,
the parser and the typesetter all changed, and the reader spends an external call.

## What changed

| area | before | after | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| what the reader is asked | `direction: "v" \| "h" \| "i"`, `angle` from the horizontal axis, between −89 and 89 | `direction` is only v/h; `lean` is degrees clockwise from upright, and `em` is one character in page pixels; both are required by the example | a tilted vertical column cannot be described by an angle from the horizontal axis in that range, and the reader answered `v` with no angle for both blocks. It still does; the pipeline no longer depends on it |
| the lean | the region's `angle` came only from the reader; 0 when absent | `leanOfBox` takes the seg plane inside the named rectangle, fits the major axis of the glyph pixels, and folds it against the page axis; a column at −85° is a 5° lean | the ink is not ambiguous. The reader's own `lean` was 0 for a 5° column and −7° for a 15.5° one, with the sign reversed |
| the frame | every entry that named a blue box got that box whole; two entries naming one box got the same 78×155 frame | entries sharing one box are split across the reading axis; the frame is a rotated rectangle whose thickness is the glyph and whose length is the column run along the lean; its page box is the rotated rectangle's bounds | the shared frame is why the two TV labels covered each other 100%. The axis-aligned width is the glyph plus the whole run's lean, which is why the font was 2–3× |
| one entry per character | six entries of `回卒業証書授` would become six labels, each translated alone | entries in one band with at most two glyphs each are folded back into one column, in reading order; a 31 split off the banner joins the column below it | the reader sometimes answers one entry per character, or splits the numeric prefix. Six labels on one banner is not a reading |
| the font | the inclined path drew `em` with a 1.2 multiplier on top of the reader's axis-aligned box | a leaned line's stroke is the glyph itself, so `fontOf` uses ×1.0; the offscreen pass passes `glyphSize` through instead of dividing by 1.2; a single column uses the run/character pitch as a floor, a merged box the narrower of em and ink | a 22 px glyph got 52 px and a 45 px glyph got 101 px. The glyph now comes from the ink, not from the box edge |
| the check | no geometry assertion in the suite | `labels[].region`, `.lean`, `.em`, `.leanTolerance`, `.emTolerance`, plus the 40% overlap rule; `fixtures/page-forty.jpg` and the `incline` case | the regression suite could not see a label that was the right words at the wrong angle and size |

What deliberately did not change:

- the erase area and the inpaint gate: only the label frame is new, and it is
  inside the rectangle the erase already covered;
- the translation prompt and the reading order;
- the grid, the crop and the blue boxes;
- the nine existing cases.

## What was left over

- **The reader's shape varies.** On the last full run it returned all three
  columns of the TV speech as one entry, "やつは熱いな…ヒヒッ一口ーモノは", so the
  translation was squeezed into one line at 17 px. On the previous run it
  returned two columns at 27 px. Both are within the 30% font tolerance, and the
  angle is measured from the ink either way, but the one-line run is the worse
  picture. Splitting a single entry back into the columns its box can hold is a
  reading/layout change, not this loop's criterion.
- **BLK 8's crop reaches past its block.** The block is 73 px wide and holds two
  columns; the crop grows to hold the blue box and shows the third column. The
  reader occasionally reads that third column into the block. The frame is built
  from the named box, so the label does not move, but the text can be longer than
  the block's own ink.
- **The 31 in the banner is horizontal, the rest is vertical.** The reader files
  the whole thing as `v`; the merged label is one leaned column. The page's
  banner reads that way, so the result is acceptable, but a block that genuinely
  mixes a horizontal number and a vertical title would need two directions.
